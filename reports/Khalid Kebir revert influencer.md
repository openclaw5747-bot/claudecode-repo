# "Irish Habibi" defies the revert label

The only identifiable public figure matching "Khalid Kebir" is **Khalid Kebir, known online as "Irish Habibi" (@irish.habibi)**, a London-based creator of **Irish and North African (Algerian) heritage** who makes short comedy and culture videos about Muslim life, and who also presents himself as founder of a strategy consultancy, **Khasib Group**, focused on the Global Islamic Economy ([TRT Next](https://next.trt.net.tr/speakers/khalid-kebir/)). He reaches **"over 1.2 million followers"** across platforms, mostly on TikTok and Instagram. The key caveat: **no verifiable source describes him as a "revert" or tells a conversion story.** His official bio rests on mixed heritage and a "third-culture upbringing", not on conversion, and his Algerian ancestry points at least as strongly toward a Muslim upbringing as toward a later conversion. The only evidence for a conversion is an unverified AI search summary of a TikTok caption, contradicted by another unverified summary in which he jokes he "converted right after birth". The identification itself is moderately confident (roughly 70%): the name, the Muslim-life content and the "blonde Muslim" theme fit, but the brief's "revert" description may come from a misreading of his appearance or from a different person no search surfaced. He has attracted almost no independent press coverage and no documented controversy. Several similarly named people, including the Irish convert Khalid Kelly and the Algerian politician Rabah Kebir, are unrelated.

## An Irish-Algerian Londoner who sells culture, not conversion

The fullest biographical source is his speaker page for TRT Next, an event run by Turkey's public broadcaster. It describes Khalid Kebir as "a content creator exploring the cultural nuances of Muslim life around the world" whose work "is informed by his **Irish and North African heritage, third-culture upbringing, and extensive international travel**" ([TRT Next](https://next.trt.net.tr/speakers/khalid-kebir/)). The page says nothing about his religion or how he came to it. His profiles reinforce the dual identity: the TikTok display name is "Irish 🇩🇿" (Algerian flag) ([TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/@irish.habibi)), and the Instagram name is "Khalid 🇩🇿☘️🇮🇪" ([Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/irish.habibi/)). The aggregator Famous Birthdays, a low-authority source, says he is of **Algerian and Irish descent and grew up in London** with his family ([Famous Birthdays](https://www.famousbirthdays.com/people/unculltured.html)). His birth name, birthplace and any former religion are not documented anywhere the researchers could reach. A UK LinkedIn profile under his name, visible only as a search snippet, lists the University of Amsterdam and carries the headline "Cultural Strategy for the $7.4T Muslim ..." ([LinkedIn](https://uk.linkedin.com/in/khalidkebir)).

## The revert premise rests on two unverified, contradictory snippets

Nothing the researchers could open says Kebir took the shahada. The only material bearing on conversion comes from AI-generated search summaries of TikTok captions that the researchers could not load, so neither claim can be reported as fact. One summary said a caption mentions "almost 6 years of being Muslim" as of July 2024, which would imply a conversion around 2018. Another summary, of his video **"Being a Blonde Muslim: My Journey in Mosques"**, said he jokes about being *asked* when he converted and quips that he "converted right after birth" ([TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/@irish.habibi/video/7393476955691158816)). That joke reads as a heritage Muslim mocking the assumption that a fair-haired Irishman must be a convert. A companion video asks viewers "What's the weirdest thing that's happened to you in the Mosque?" ([TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/@irish.habibi/video/7393107831190326560)).

The weight of evidence leans against the revert label. His self-description emphasises ancestry rather than a journey; his content includes Algerian and Amazigh identity commentary that only makes sense from an insider ([TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/@irish.habibi/video/6967771208741915909)); and no revert-story video by him was found. A TikTok titled "irish guy Revert story to Islam" does exist, but it was posted by a different account, @no_contradiction, and nothing confirms it shows Kebir ([TikTok @no_contradiction](https://www.tiktok.com/@no_contradiction/video/7371003424818695456)). The most plausible explanation is that audiences read his visibly Irish appearance and "blonde Muslim" jokes as a conversion story. The alternative, that the brief refers to some other revert influencer whose name was garbled, cannot be ruled out, but searches for "Khaled Kebir", "Khalid Kabir" and "Khalid Kebeer" with revert or convert terms found no such person. Until a primary source surfaces, such as his own video describing a conversion, he should be described as a Muslim-life creator of mixed heritage, not as a revert.

## Seven hundred thousand TikTok followers built on Muslim-life comedy

TikTok is his core platform. A direct check on 10 November 2026 showed **about 709,400 followers, 38.4 million likes and 278 videos**, with the bio "For the culturally curious 🌍🍉🕋" and a link to a halal travel guide ([TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/@irish.habibi)). Instagram stands at **about 513K followers** and 350 posts per a cached snapshot ([Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/irish.habibi/)), and Threads at **about 65K**, where recent posts draw only single-digit likes ([Threads](https://www.threads.com/@irish.habibi)). Those three figures sum to roughly 1.28 million, which matches TRT's "over 1.2 million" and suggests it is a cross-platform total. One researcher found a YouTube channel, @irishhabibi, linked to the same Instagram account ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/@irishhabibi)); another found none, so treat YouTube as a minor channel. No X account was found.

The content is short-form comedy and cultural commentary. Famous Birthdays says he posted his first TikTok on **25 December 2020** and that his best-known post, a **26 February 2023 skit about Muslim couples fighting, drew about 5.4 million views** ([Famous Birthdays](https://www.famousbirthdays.com/people/unculltured.html)). Representative titles include the ruqyah joke "Akhi trying to take the jinn out" ([TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/@irish.habibi/video/7416459469657230625)), "Algerian Amazigh Khalid Uncultured Music Review" ([TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/@irish.habibi/video/6982259300379004161)), "Emotional Farewell: A Syrian Friend's Dilemma" ([TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/@irish.habibi/video/7447659593699822881)), a family-faith post, "I want my Kids Jannah to be under your feet" ([TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/@irish.habibi/video/7319876460066442529)), and political commentary, "It's only freedom on their terms" ([TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/@irish.habibi/video/7515187357629353238)). The Famous Birthdays URL slug "unculltured" hints at an earlier handle, echoed in the "Khalid Uncultured" video title. Since 2026 his output has shifted toward **halal travel**: his Linktree sells guides for Bosnia and Georgia, "Travel Halal Anywhere" and "The 7 Wonders of the Islamic World" ([Linktree](https://linktr.ee/irishhabibi)), and his Threads feed features Georgian mountain trips ([Threads](https://www.threads.com/@irish.habibi)). No street-da'wah or revert-journey content turned up anywhere.

## A consultancy pitched at the $7.4 trillion Muslim market

Beyond the travel guides, his commercial identity is consulting. TRT describes him as a "**London-based market intelligence expert and founder of Khasib Group**", a strategic advisory firm connecting institutions with the Global Islamic Economy, and as **lead author of *Cultural Currency 2026***, advising C-suite executives, private equity firms and policymakers on cultural trends and demographic change ([TRT Next](https://next.trt.net.tr/speakers/khalid-kebir/)). The LinkedIn headline frames the same pitch around the "$7.4T Muslim" economy ([LinkedIn](https://uk.linkedin.com/in/khalidkebir)). Neither Khasib Group nor *Cultural Currency 2026* has any independent web presence the researchers could find, so both rest on self-supplied descriptions. No brand deals, charity partnerships or named creator collaborations were verified. The trajectory suggests a creator turning his audience into credibility for a professional advisory brand.

## Near-silence from the press, and one viral Gaza clip

Independent reception is thin. No Reddit threads, forum discussions, podcast interviews or profiles in Muslim outlets such as 5Pillars or MuslimMatters were found, and round-ups of rising Arab and Muslim comedians did not name him ([The National](https://thenational-the-national-prod.cdn.arcpublishing.com/arts-culture/pop-culture/2023/01/31/up-and-coming-arab-comedians-to-follow-on-social-media); [Hyphen](https://hyphenonline.com/2022/06/07/7-up-and-coming-muslim-comedians-to-watch-out-for/)). The only notable moment came in **November 2023**, when Turkish outlet EHA Medya shared a clip of "social media phenomenon Khalid Kebir" mocking the Israeli claim that an Arabic calendar at Gaza's Rantisi Children's Hospital listed "the names of terrorists" ([EHA Medya on X](https://x.com/eha_medya/status/1725279662614495450)). A separate 2025 Middle East Eye story about an "Irish TikTok content creator" rebuking Netanyahu over Gaza hospitals refers to the creator as "her" and is **not about him** ([Aliran / MEE](https://m.aliran.com/web-specials/you-attacked-every-hospital-in-gaza-irish-tiktok-creator-on-netanyahus-criticism-of-iran)). No controversy, theological criticism, "fake revert" accusation or criticism of his business model was documented. Arabic, French and Turkish coverage was not searched in depth.

## Conclusion

The "revert influencer" framing probably says more about how audiences read a fair-haired Irish-Algerian Muslim than about Khalid Kebir's own story. His brand is built on being an insider who translates Muslim and North African diaspora life with humour, and now on selling that cultural fluency to travellers and institutional clients. Calling him a revert without a primary source risks misrepresenting a heritage Muslim, which his own "converted right after birth" joke appears to push back against.

Two checks would settle the open questions: viewing his own captions and Instagram highlights for any statement about when he became Muslim, and finding any independent record of Khasib Group and *Cultural Currency 2026*. Until then, his follower numbers are the best-documented fact about him, while his religious biography and his consulting credentials both rest on self-description.
