# Hajj on Horseback expedition
*Researched 3 Nov 2026 · 23 sources across web ×2, YouTube, Reddit, news, forums/social, official · updated with LaunchGood and Leeds screening checks*

## TL;DR
- Three Spanish Muslims rode Arabian endurance mares from southern Spain to Saudi Arabia and performed Hajj in June 2025: Abdelkader Harkassi Aidi, Abdallah Rafael Hernández Mancha and Tarek Rodríguez. The riders described it as reviving an Andalusian pilgrimage route that had not been used for more than 500 years [1][2][3].
- The trip is finished. They left Almonaster la Real (Huelva) on 13 Oct 2024 and crossed into Saudi Arabia on 2 May 2025 [3][6]. Saudi authorities did not allow the horses into Makkah [2][4].
- About €56k was raised online, roughly 28% of the €200,000 goal: €50,015 from 1,118 supporters on LaunchGood [22] plus €6,204 on GoFundMe [9]. No sponsor took the €5k or €10k sponsorship tiers [22]. The rest of the support came from the riders' savings, donations along the route and a Saudi influencer [2][4].
- In 2026 the project is about telling the story, not riding. A documentary was advertised for screening in Leeds on Saturday 18 July (2026 by the page's dates), with a Q&A with Harkassi [12]. Harkassi gave a talk at Muslim LitFest London on 18 Oct 2026, and a book is announced as "forthcoming" [11].

## Key findings

**Who and why.** Hernández is a retired history teacher who converted to Islam around 1990. He vowed to make Hajj on horseback "the way his Andalusian ancestors did" [2][10]. Harkassi organised the trip and acted as spokesperson [2][9][22]. The stated aims were to keep that vow, revive the route, show "that Spanish Muslims still exist" and promote Muslim unity [4][5]. The riders cite 1491 as the last time a pilgrim used this route, and Omar Patún's 1491–95 journey as the historical precedent [1][4]. This rests on the riders' own claims and is well supported across outlets.

**Route and logistics.**
- **Route:** Spain → France → Italy → Slovenia → Croatia → Bosnia → Serbia → Bulgaria (crossed by car) → Türkiye → Syria → Jordan → Saudi Arabia [1][4]. They skipped North Africa because the Morocco–Algeria border is closed and Libya is unstable [5].
- **Milestones:** Istanbul in late February 2025, where they spent Ramadan [7]. Syria in April, after Assad's fall opened the route [3]. The Al-Haditha/Qurayyat border on 2 May 2025 [6][8].
- **Training and horses:** Before the trip they did training rides of 750 km in 2021, 650 km in 2022 and 450 km in 2023–24 [9][10][22]. They trained for about four years with Arabian mares of Khuzestani bloodlines [2].
- **Pace and support:** They averaged about 40 km a day, with a support car that drove ahead [2].
- **Borders:** Horses count as livestock, so every border needed import paperwork. Hernández called this "the biggest challenge" [6]. Their own mares couldn't enter Bosnia, so they used borrowed Bosnian horses there and got their own back in Türkiye [4][13].

**Funding.**
- **LaunchGood:** €50,015 raised of a €200,000 goal, from 1,118 supporters [22].
  - Most donations were small: the €5, €10 and €20 tiers had 381 claims between them.
  - Both sponsorship tiers (€5k Gold and €10k Platinum) show 0 claimed.
  - The page's countdown has run out, so the campaign looks closed.
- **GoFundMe:** €6,204 from 51 donations [9].
- **Self-funding:** each rider put in €1,500, and that money was gone before northern Spain [2].
- **Along the route:** more than €1,200 came from mosque-goers in Navarre and more than €2,000 from Novi Pazar. A Saudi Snapchat influencer donated a caravan [2][4].

**Setbacks.**
- A vet retired two horses, and that ended fourth rider Mohamed Mesbahi's ride [2].
- Horses got lost in Bosnia and turned up unharmed in a minefield [1].
- Snow in the Alps and a broken-down support car [4].

**Saudi end of the ride.** The riders got a warm welcome: medical checks, paid flights to Madinah and a meeting with the Emir of Madinah [2][4][8]. But they were told to leave the horses near Riyadh. Hernández's take: "They didn't want 50,000 people coming on horseback next year" [4]. They still needed standard Hajj permits. In 2025, performing Hajj without one could bring a fine of up to SR20,000 [17].

**Media and legacy.**
- **Documentary:** the crowdfunding pages promised one, with donors named in the credits [9][22]. The Leeds New Muslims screening is the only evidence found that it exists in some form. That page gives no title or venue, and it doesn't confirm the event took place [12].
- **Book:** described as "forthcoming" at LitFest [11]. LaunchGood donors at the €200 tier were promised a digital travel diary or e-book [22].
- **Video coverage:** TRT World, The National, BBC Indonesia and Islam Channel all covered the ride [13][14]. The official YouTube channel posts mostly short clips after the Hajj, and its last upload was 29 Dec 2025 [13].

## What people are saying
- **The online following was large.** Reported figures are about 345–351K Instagram followers and about 250–275K on TikTok [15][20][21]. These come from search snippets and a repost of the NYT piece, so they are unverified. LaunchGood's 1,118 supporters show that a small share of followers turned into donors [22].
- **Reception in the Muslim world was overwhelmingly positive.** There were crowds in Bosnia, Türkiye and Syria, where the riders were hailed as "descendants of the Umayyads", plus receptions with officials [4][5].
- **Spanish domestic coverage was thin, which disappointed the riders** [4].
- **No Reddit, HN, X or Substack discussion was found.** Reddit itself couldn't be opened directly, so this is a gap in the evidence, not a sign of no reaction. No critical voices surfaced anywhere, including on animal welfare.

## Points of disagreement / uncertainty
- **Distance:** 6,500 km [2], about 7,000 km [5][11] or 8,000 km [1][6]. The difference probably reflects legs driven or ridden on borrowed horses.
- **Number of riders:** 3 in most coverage. Gulf News and EFE say 4 [3][8], probably counting Mesbahi or support driver Bouchaib Jadil. The Instagram bio says "6 riders – 6 horses – 14 countries" [15].
- **What happened to the horses:** The National says there are plans to preserve their bloodlines in Saudi Arabia [2]. A snippet from Daily Sabah (Mar 2026) says they were sold [16]. This is unresolved.
- **The official site is out of date.** It still reads as a project raising money and has a dead donate link. It cites "reg. no. 028347" as a Spanish non-profit, which I couldn't verify [10].
- **The book has no confirmed publisher, title or date.** Beacon Books sponsored the LitFest talk, but that doesn't make it the publisher [11].

## Bottom line
Hajj on Horseback was a real expedition, completed in 2025: a seven-month ride from Andalusia to Saudi Arabia. It was paid for by about €56k raised online from more than 1,100 small donors, plus the riders' own money and support along the route. No corporate sponsor came forward. In late 2026 the project is about telling the story through a documentary screening, talks and a forthcoming book. The horses' fate and the exact distance remain unclear.
